# Matching approach

The recommender first removes ineligible candidates: profiles must be active, verified adults of the opposite gender with consent to share. Both people's stated age ranges, marital-status preferences, and non-smoking dealbreakers must hold. Conflicting definite views on children are excluded, as are different-city pairs where neither person can relocate. For male customers, the exercise's four additional rules are hard constraints: candidates must be younger, shorter, earn less, and state exactly the same intention about children. Female customers have no income or height heuristic; their recommendations emphasise values, life plans, career context, and relocation feasibility. Rules never relax themselves to fill a results page.

Eligible candidates receive two directional scores across values, children, location, lifestyle, career context, language, and profile-text similarity. The final score is their harmonic mean, which penalises one-sided fit. The AI component is an explainable content-based recommender: TF-IDF converts bios to weighted word vectors and cosine similarity measures shared vocabulary. It contributes 12 of 100 points rather than dominating the result. Every suggestion shows its reasons, points to discuss, directional scores, and factor contributions. An optional server-side OpenAI Responses API integration drafts a personalised email from a minimal set of profile facts and fit reasons; it cannot change eligibility or scores. The matchmaker reviews every draft. The live static demo uses the content-based recommender and a local template; the OpenAI button appears only on the Node deployment when a key is configured.

This MVP assumes a single matchmaker, an opposite-gender pool, INR income in lakh per year, centimetres for height, and ages derived from dates of birth. It contains 12 assigned customers and 240 deterministic fictional profiles: 120 men and 120 women. Verification and consent are simulated flags. A Maybe response receives partial credit and a discussion prompt; definite child-plan conflicts fail eligibility, while male-customer child plans must match exactly. Caste, religion, photos, names, and absolute wealth never contribute to ranking. Career similarity provides conversational context, not a measure of professional prestige. Weights and score labels are hand-authored, not trained or empirically calibrated; scores are compatibility indices rather than probabilities of relationship success. Production would need real consent and identity checks, individual preference controls, durable access-controlled storage, and outcome-based calibration before any claim of predictive accuracy.
